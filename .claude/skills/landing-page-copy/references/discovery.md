# Discovery

What a converting page needs to know, what to do when the user does not know it, and how to ask without exhausting them.

## The rule

Mine the brief first. Ask only what is missing. Batch the questions. Guess what is guessable and label the guess.

The user came with an idea and energy. Twelve questions before a single line of copy kills both. If you can write a decent draft with three flagged assumptions, do that instead - a concrete draft provokes far better corrections than a questionnaire does ("no, no es eso, es más bien...").

## Must have before writing

You genuinely cannot write the page without these five. If they are missing from the brief, ask.

1. **The reader.** Who specifically. Not "mujeres 35-55" but where she is in her life right now: what her week looks like, what she has already tried, what she tells herself about why she has not done this yet.
2. **The pain, in her words.** The sentence she would actually say. This is the single most valuable input; ask for it in free text and use her phrasing nearly verbatim in the copy. Real language beats copywriter language every time.
3. **The transformation.** What is different afterwards. Ask for a concrete change, not a feeling: "¿qué hace o nota esta persona el martes siguiente que antes no hacía?"
4. **The action.** What exactly the page asks for: email, booking, payment, WhatsApp, call. And what happens right after they do it - the copy has to promise the next step truthfully.
5. **The offer's hard facts.** Format, date, place, duration, price, capacity. If any is undecided, the page still needs a placeholder marked as such, because a missing price is a conversion hole.

## Ask if relevant

6. **Proof.** Testimonials (verbatim, with names), previous editions, numbers, credentials, press. Ask explicitly whether you may quote them and whether the wording can be edited. If there is none, say so plainly and compensate with founder story and specificity - never fabricate.
7. **Objections.** The real reasons people say no. Ask: "¿qué te han dicho las personas que se lo pensaron y al final no vinieron?" That answer is worth more than a whole FAQ you invent.
8. **The competition / alternatives.** What she would do instead, including doing nothing. PASTOR's Amplify beat needs this, and it sharpens every other framework.
9. **Urgency.** Real deadline, real limited spots, real price change. Ask if it exists; if it does not, do not manufacture one.
10. **Risk reversal.** Refunds, guarantees, what happens if she cannot come. Even a modest true policy converts.
11. **Voice and constraints.** Who narrates (yo / nosotras), tú vs usted, words to avoid, claims that are off-limits, brand terms with fixed spelling.
12. **Traffic source.** Newsletter, Instagram ad, word of mouth. Cold traffic needs more setup and more proof; warm traffic needs less problem and more offer.
13. **Length and placement.** Standalone page or section of an existing one? Any word count they have in mind?

## How to ask

Use the AskUserQuestion tool for **choices**: conversion goal, framework, page length, tone, traffic source. Options with a one-line consequence each.

Use **free text** for the raw material: the pain, the objections, testimonial quotes, the transformation. Multiple choice on these throws away exactly what makes the copy specific. Ask them together, in one message, numbered:

```
Cuatro cosas y escribo:

1. ¿Qué frase diría ella sobre cómo se siente ahora? (sus palabras, aunque sea larga)
2. ¿Qué te han dicho las que se lo pensaron y no vinieron?
3. ¿Tienes algún testimonio de ediciones anteriores? Pégalo tal cual.
4. Precio, fecha, lugar, plazas.
```

## When they do not know

Common, especially for questions 1, 3 and 7. Do not stall.

- **Propose and confirm.** "Voy a asumir que el 'no' más frecuente es el precio, y trato esa objeción en la sección 6. Si en realidad es el miedo a ir sola, dímelo y cambio esa sección." One line, they answer in three words.
- **Draft two versions of the risky section** rather than asking again. Cheaper for them to pick than to introspect.
- **Flag it in the doc.** The "Supuestos y huecos" section at the end exists for this. Every assumption listed there is a decision the user can reverse in ten seconds.

## When the brief is a rewrite of an existing page

Read the existing page first, then diagnose before rewriting. Say which of these it is failing, because the fix differs:

- No clear single action (three competing CTAs, or none)
- Describes the thing instead of what it does for the reader
- Starts with "sobre mí" instead of with them
- No proof anywhere
- Practical facts missing or buried
- Headline that could belong to any page in the sector
- Objections never addressed
- Copy is fine but the page never asks

Then keep what works. If a line is already the reader's own language, or already converts, do not improve it into blandness. Say which lines you kept and why.
