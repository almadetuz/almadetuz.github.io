## Headlines

The headline is most of the page. It is read by everyone; the second paragraph is read by a fraction. Spend disproportionate effort here and always deliver three variants across different angles.

**Angles that work:**

| Angle | Shape | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Pain, named | Say the thing she has not said out loud | "Llevas tanto tiempo cuidando a todos que ya no sabes qué te gusta" |
| Transformation | The after-state, concrete | "Un sábado entero en el que nadie te necesita" |
| Curious question | Opens a loop she wants closed | "¿Y si la mejor manera de desconectar fuera redescubrir lo que nuestras abuelas ya sabían?" |
| Specific promise | Number, time, mechanism | "Cantar sin saber cantar: un día para encontrar tu voz" |
| Contradiction | Two things that should not go together | "No es un curso de costura. No vas a aprender a coser." |

**Tests a headline must pass:**

- Could it head a competitor's page? Then it says nothing. Specificity is the fix - a detail only this offer can claim.
- Does it contain a word she uses, or only words the sector uses? "Espacio de transformación consciente" is sector language. "Un día para ti sola" is hers.
- Read it out loud. If you stumble, she stumbles.

**Las 4Us - how to choose between your three variants.** Score each one on urgente (¿hay una razón real para leerlo hoy?), único (¿podría firmarlo otra página?), útil (¿se ve lo que ella gana?) y ultra-específico (¿hay un detalle que sólo esta oferta puede dar?).

Use it as a scoring test, not as a recipe: a headline that hits two Us hard beats one that hits all four weakly, and forcing urgency where there is none is exactly the manufactured pressure to avoid. Ultra-especificidad is the U that rescues most weak headlines - "un sábado" → "un sábado de 10 a 19 en una casa de Malasaña".

Avoid: the product name as headline, "Bienvenida a...", abstract nouns stacked ("conexión, presencia y sanación"), and anything that needs the subhead to make sense.

The subhead earns its place by adding the concrete thing the headline was too short to carry: the who, the when, or the mechanism. Do not restate the headline in longer words.

## Body copy

**Write to one person.** Second person singular, one reader, one situation. "Muchas mujeres sienten que..." puts her in a crowd; "sientes que..." puts her in the sentence.

**Specificity is the whole game.** Every abstraction has a concrete version hiding behind it, and the concrete version is more persuasive and more memorable:

- "te ayudará a relajarte" → "a las dos horas se te olvida mirar el móvil"
- "un ambiente acogedor" → "una casa con luz, quince sillas en círculo y té toda la tarde"
- "grupo reducido" → "quince personas, ni una más"

When you catch yourself writing an adjective, ask what you saw that made you choose it, and write that instead.

**Use their words.** Anything the user gave you in the reader's own phrasing goes in nearly verbatim. Do not upgrade her vocabulary.

**Earn each paragraph.** Every paragraph's job is to get the next one read. Cut anything that is true but does not move her - especially your own credentials in the first third.

**Story beats explanation.** A three-line scene ("llegó sin hablar con nadie y a media mañana estaba cantando") outperforms a paragraph explaining that the atmosphere is welcoming.

## Benefits vs features

A feature is what the thing is. A benefit is what it does for her. Pages fail by listing features and assuming the leap.

The move - **FAB** (feature, advantage, benefit): state the feature, then what makes it different from the alternative, then ask "¿y eso qué le da?" until the answer is emotional. Only the last rung goes in the bullet; the middle two are working notes.

The advantage rung is the one people skip, and it is where differentiation lives: "música en vivo" is a feature, "no es una grabación" is why it beats every other workshop she has been to.

| Feature | So what | Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| Todo el día, de 10 a 19 | No hay prisa | Por una vez no miras el reloj |
| Música en vivo | No es una grabación | El sonido te atraviesa el cuerpo, no lo escuchas: lo sientes |
| Grupo de 15 | Te ven | No eres una más en una sala |
| Sin nivel previo | No te van a evaluar | Nadie va a juzgar lo que hagas, tampoco tú |

In bullets, lead with the benefit and let the feature follow in the same line. A list of six benefits is the most-read block on most pages after the hero, so make each one a full thought, not two words.

**Collar de perlas:** write the list so every item stands alone as a complete reason to say yes. Nobody reads a list top to bottom - she lands somewhere in the middle, and whichever pearl she lands on has to be able to sell on its own. Test it by reading any single bullet cold: if it only makes sense after the one above it, it is a chain link, not a pearl. Order them so the strongest is first and the second strongest last.

## Objections

Objections are where the sale is lost, quietly. The reader never tells you; she just leaves.

Handle them in her framing, not yours. The FAQ format works because it lets her see her own doubt in print, which is itself reassuring:

```
**¿Es necesario saber cantar?**
Para nada.
...
```

Rules: answer the real question, not the flattering version of it. Be brief. Do not get defensive ("Mucha gente piensa erróneamente que..."). If the objection is legitimate for some readers, say so and let them out - "si buscas aprender técnica vocal, esto no es para ti" makes every other answer more credible.

Price objections are rarely about money; they are about certainty of value. Answer with specificity (what the day contains) and risk reversal, not with a discount.

## CTAs and form microcopy

**One action per page.** Repeated, never varied. If a page asks for an email in one place and a WhatsApp in another, both underperform.

**Button text is first person and specific.** What she gets, not what she does:

- Weak: "Enviar", "Más información", "Apúntate"
- Strong: "Sí, quiero recibirlas", "Quiero apuntarme", "Descubrir el Tüz", "Guárdame una plaza"

**Around the form**, three pieces do the work:
- Title: what she is signing up for
- Subtitle: the practical facts (cuándo, dónde, precio, plazas) - this is where they belong on email-capture pages
- Line underneath: the reassurance that removes the last hesitation - "Darse de alta es gratis, darse de baja también", "No te voy a escribir cada día", "Te contamos lo que hay que hacer para venir"

**Say what happens next.** "Te llega un email con los detalles y desde ahí decides." Uncertainty about the next step stops more people than price.

**Repeat the CTA** every 400-600 words on long pages, and always immediately after the strongest emotional beat - right after the story, right after the testimonial - not only at the bottom.

## Rhythm and typography

Short sentences. Then one longer one that lets the reader breathe and follows the thought where it wants to go. Then short again.

One idea per paragraph. Two or three lines maximum on mobile, which is where she is reading.

Line breaks mid-thought are a voice tool, not sloppiness - Alma de Tüz pages use them heavily and they slow the reader down in the right places:

```
Cuando todo estaba perdido,\
**mi cable a tierra** fueron palabras y versos de otros.
```

**Bold** the three or four phrases per screen that carry the meaning, so the skimmer who reads only the bold still gets the argument. Bolding everything bolds nothing.

Headings are copy too. A reader who scrolls the page reading only H2s and H3s should still receive the whole pitch.

## Spanish specifics

- **es-ES, `tú`.** Never `usted`. Never `vosotros/as` addressed to one reader.
- **Inclusive language:** on this site the reader is usually addressed in feminine ("sola", "plena", "cansada") because the audience is women. Confirm before defaulting either way; do not use "@" or "x" forms, they break the intimate register and screen readers.
- Watch the Anglicism drift in copywriting: "descubre el poder de", "transforma tu vida", "lleva tu X al siguiente nivel" are translated ad-speak and read as generic.
- Punctuation: use `¿` `¡` properly, `«»` or `"` consistently, and the ellipsis for the trailing thought the voice is built on.
- Em-dashes are not part of this voice; use commas, line breaks, or parentheses.

## SEO fields

Front matter on this site: `title`, `seo_description`, `seo_image`. Give all of them.

- `title`: the offer's real name, short. It shows in the tab and in shares.
- `seo_description`: max ~155 characters, written as copy, not as a summary. It is a headline in a search result - it should make someone click, and it should contain what the thing is and for whom. Existing example: "Espacio de arteterapia creativa: costura, voz, cuerpo y música en vivo".
- Slug: short, readable, hyphenated, no dates unless the page is edition-specific (`coser-y-cantar/madrid-16-nov`).

## Failure modes

Check the draft against these before shipping:

1. **Adjective soup.** "Un espacio íntimo, transformador y profundo." Nothing is visible. Replace with one scene.
2. **The page never asks.** Beautiful copy, no CTA, or a CTA so soft it reads as optional.
3. **About-me-first.** Her problem before your story, always. Your story is proof, and proof comes after recognition.
4. **Feature dump.** Duration, materials, capacity, with no translation into what she gets.
5. **Buried price.** If it costs money, say so where she is looking.
6. **Manufactured urgency.** Fake countdowns and invented scarcity. They also poison the real ones you use later.
7. **Framework showing.** Headings named "Problema", "Solución". The reader should feel the sequence, not see it.
8. **Overpromising.** Especially on health and wellbeing. First-person lived experience ("a mí me salvó") is honest; a promise of outcome is not.
9. **Uniform length.** Every paragraph the same size flattens the page. Vary it.
10. **Vague CTA microcopy.** "Enviar" on a button is a missed conversion.
