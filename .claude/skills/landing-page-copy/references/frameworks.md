# Frameworks

Read the section for the framework you chose. Each one gives the beats, the section map, and the traps.

A framework is a sequence of emotional states, not a set of headings. The reader should never be able to tell you used one. If a section only exists because the acronym demanded a letter, cut it.

## Choosing

| Signal in the brief | Framework |
|---|---|
| Offer is new or needs explaining; audience is warm but not desperate | AIDA |
| One sharp, urgent, nameable pain; cheap or low-commitment offer; short page | PAS |
| High price, high commitment, long deliberation; strong story and real testimonials available | PASTOR |
| The offer's value is a visible transformation, or the audience does not know a better state is possible | BAB |
| Plausible offer, but a reader who has been let down before and needs proof before the ask | AIDCA |
| Warm audience, quiet pain, a page that must not feel like selling | 4Ps |
| A cause or a call to participate, where the reader is not yet in pain | ACCA |
| One vivid protagonist object or place; video and webinar scripts | SCH |

The first four are the workhorses; read [Secondary frameworks](#secondary-frameworks) for the rest. FAB, las 4Us and el collar de perlas are not page structures at all - they are [section-level tools](#section-level-formulas).

Secondary considerations:

- **Cold traffic** (ads, first visit) needs more agitation and more proof: PAS or PASTOR.
- **Warm traffic** (they already know you, came from your newsletter) needs less problem-setup and more offer clarity: AIDA or BAB, shorter.
- **No social proof yet**: avoid PASTOR (the T is a whole beat). Use PAS or BAB and carry the credibility with the founder story and concrete specificity.
- **Email capture, not sale**: shorten whatever you pick. You are asking for an email, not 300€. Over-selling a free thing reads as suspicious.

Frameworks combine. A BAB hero on top of a PAS body is common and fine. Say what you did.

## AIDA

Attention, Interest, Desire, Action. The default. Works when you need to explain something before anyone can want it.

**Beats:**

1. **Attention** - Hero. Headline that stops the scroll: a specific promise, a sharp question, or a tension. Not the name of the product. Subhead adds the concrete detail the headline earned the right to give.
2. **Interest** - Now that they stopped, keep them. This is where the pain lives: describe their current situation so accurately they feel read. Interest comes from recognition, not from information about you.
3. **Desire** - Move from "this is my problem" to "I want this". Benefits, not features: what the day/product/experience actually gives them. Sensory and concrete. Proof lands here (testimonial, story, credential) because desire without belief does not convert.
4. **Action** - One ask, unambiguous, with the friction removed. What happens after they click, what it costs, what they need to bring.

**Typical section map (medium page, ~700-1000 words):**

| # | Section | Beat |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hero: H1 + subhead + CTA | A |
| 2 | El problema / dónde estás ahora | I |
| 3 | Qué es esto (la propuesta en 3 líneas) | I→D |
| 4 | Qué te llevas (beneficios, 4-6 bullets) | D |
| 5 | Prueba: testimonio o historia | D |
| 6 | Detalles prácticos: cuándo, dónde, precio | D→A |
| 7 | Objeciones / preguntas frecuentes | A |
| 8 | CTA final | A |

**Traps:** the Interest section drifting into "sobre mí" - it is about them, you come later. And Desire sections that list features (duración, materiales, aforo) instead of what those features do for the reader.

## PAS

Problem, Agitation, Solution. Fast, sharp, uncomfortable in a productive way. Best short.

**Beats:**

1. **Problem** - Name it in one sentence, in their words. If they would not say this sentence out loud about themselves, you have the wrong problem.
2. **Agitation** - Follow the problem forward in time and outward in cost. Not "you are tired" repeated louder, but: what it is costing, where it shows up, what it will look like in a year unchanged. Concrete scenes beat adjectives. This is the beat that does the work; it is also the one that goes wrong most often (see the ethics note below).
3. **Solution** - Arrive as relief, not as a sales pitch. Introduce the offer as the thing that resolves the specific agitation you just described - point by point, in the same order. Then the ask.

**Typical section map (short page, ~400-700 words):**

| # | Section | Beat |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hero: the problem as headline | P |
| 2 | Lo que te está costando | A |
| 3 | La salida: esto es lo que propongo | S |
| 4 | Qué incluye + práctico | S |
| 5 | CTA + una objeción resuelta | S |

**Ethics of agitation:** you are reflecting a pain the reader already carries. Two lines you do not cross - do not manufacture a pain they did not have, and do not blame them for the one they do have. "Llevas años posponiéndote" is recognition. "Si siguieras así te lo mereces" is contempt. Recognition converts; contempt makes them close the tab and feel bad about you.

Keep agitation to about a third of the page. Past that it stops being empathy and starts being wallowing, and the reader gets tired before you offer them anything.

## PASTOR

Problem, Amplify, Story, Testimonials, Offer, Response. Long-form. Use when the price or the commitment is high enough that the reader needs to know and trust you before deciding.

**Beats:**

1. **Problem** - As PAS, but with room. Show you understand the problem better than they can articulate it.
2. **Amplify** - The cost of not solving it. Include the cost of the alternatives they have already tried (the app, the book, the other course) - "por qué lo de siempre no te funcionó" is one of the highest-value passages on any long page, because it explains their past failure without blaming them.
3. **Story** - Yours or a client's. The arc that matters: I was where you are → what I found → what changed. Specific details make it true (dates, places, objects, the thing your friend said). This beat carries the credibility that a stranger's page cannot otherwise have.
4. **Testimonials** - Real ones, with names. Placed to answer the specific doubt that arises right there, not dumped in a grid. Best testimonials narrate a change ("llegué pensando X, salí con Y"), not a compliment ("todo genial").
5. **Offer** - Now, and only now, the thing itself. Break it down completely: what happens, in what order, what is included, what it costs, what it does not include. Ambiguity at this beat kills long pages - by here the reader wants to say yes and is looking for reasons to trust the details.
6. **Response** - The ask, plus everything that removes risk: guarantee, what happens next, deadline (if real), and the last objection. Close by restating the transformation, not the price.

**Typical section map (long page, 1500-3000+ words):**

| # | Section | Beat |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hero | P |
| 2 | Dónde estás ahora | P |
| 3 | Lo que te cuesta / por qué no funcionó lo anterior | A |
| 4 | Mi historia | S |
| 5 | CTA intermedio | - |
| 6 | Historia de una participante / testimonio largo | T |
| 7 | Qué es esto exactamente | O |
| 8 | Cómo es el día, hora por hora | O |
| 9 | Qué incluye / qué no | O |
| 10 | Testimonios cortos | T |
| 11 | Precio, plazas, fechas | O |
| 12 | Preguntas frecuentes = objeciones | R |
| 13 | CTA final + qué pasa después | R |

Repeat the CTA every 400-600 words on a page this long. Same promise, same words, every time - varying the button text between "Quiero apuntarme" and "Más información" makes the reader re-decide what they are agreeing to.

**Trap:** the story eating the page. Story is a beat, not the purpose. If the reader finishes moved but unclear on what is being offered and what it costs, the page failed.

## BAB

Before, After, Bridge. The shortest path when the value is a transformation.

**Beats:**

1. **Before** - Their world today, told in scenes, not adjectives. Specific enough that they think "me está mirando".
2. **After** - The same life, changed. Present tense, sensory, and *believable* - one notch beyond where they are, not a fantasy. Overreach here ("nunca más volverás a sentir ansiedad") destroys the page: it is both untrue and unbelievable.
3. **Bridge** - Your offer as the mechanism that crosses between them. It must be plausible: state briefly *how* it works, because a bridge nobody can see the structure of does not get walked on. Then the ask.

**Typical section map (short-medium, ~500-900 words):**

| # | Section | Beat |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hero: the After as headline, or the Before as a question | B/A |
| 2 | Cómo es ahora | B |
| 3 | Cómo podría ser | A |
| 4 | Qué hay entre una cosa y la otra: esto | Bridge |
| 5 | Cómo funciona (mecanismo, 3 pasos) | Bridge |
| 6 | Prueba: alguien que lo cruzó | Bridge |
| 7 | Práctico + CTA | Bridge |

**Trap:** an After written as vague uplift ("te sentirás plena, libre, conectada"). Adjective soup is invisible. Write a scene: what she does on the Tuesday after, what she notices in her body, what she stops doing.

## Secondary frameworks

The four above cover most pages. These cover the cases they handle badly. Same rule applies: the reader must never be able to see the acronym.

### AIDCA

Atención, Interés, Deseo, **Convencimiento**, Acción. AIDA with a proof beat wedged in before the ask.

Use it when the offer sounds plausible but the reader has been disappointed before - the "otro taller más de bienestar" reflex. At that point one piece of evidence placed right before the button converts better than another paragraph of desire.

The C is not a testimonial grid. It is the single piece of proof that answers the doubt the Desire section just raised: if Desire promised "vas a cantar aunque creas que no sabes", Convencimiento is the woman who says "yo no cantaba".

**Beats:** as AIDA, but between Desire and Action insert one proof block - a testimonial, a real number, a photo of the previous edition, the fact that it has run four times. Then close.

**Trap:** proof placed too early. Evidence before desire answers a question the reader has not asked yet, and reads as insecurity.

### ACCA

Awareness (conciencia), Comprensión, Convicción, Acción. Built for causes rather than offers: the reader does not know the problem exists, or knows and does not feel implicated.

**Beats:**

1. **Awareness** - state the situation as fact. Not her pain: a reality she had not looked at.
2. **Comprensión** - make it hers. Why it touches her, who it affects, what it looks like up close. Empathy, not statistics.
3. **Convicción** - it is solvable, and her acting moves it. This is where a cause page usually stops too early.
4. **Acción** - one concrete step, small and immediate.

Use for a benefit concert, a collaboration, a call for participation - any page whose ask is joining rather than buying. Avoid it when the reader arrives already in pain: PAS gets there faster.

**Trap:** staying in Awareness. Information moves nobody; the two C beats do the work.

### 4Ps

Picture, Promise, Prove, Push. The narrative cousin of AIDA - it opens by putting the reader inside a scene instead of naming a pain, so it sells without feeling like selling.

**Beats:**

1. **Picture** - a scene she can stand inside. Present tense, sensory, hers or yours.
2. **Promise** - what this offers, stated plainly, once the scene has done the wanting for you.
3. **Prove** - why it is true: story, testimonial, specificity, track record.
4. **Push** - the ask, with the real reason to do it now.

This is the closest of any framework here to the Alma de Tüz voice. When the audience is warm and the pain is real but quiet - a newsletter list, people who already know Amanda - 4Ps beats PAS, which would agitate a reader who came in calm.

**Trap:** a Picture with no tension. A pleasant scene she has no stake in is a postcard, and she scrolls past it.

### SCH

Star, Chain, Hook. Estrella (the protagonist: the offer, the place, a person), cadena (facts and benefits linked so each earns the next), gancho (the ask).

Use for video and webinar scripts, or a page built around one vivid object - an instrument, a house, a garment. It is a weak choice for a general landing page: without the discipline of a Desire beat the Chain slides into a feature list.

## Blocks any page needs

Whatever framework you chose, a converting page has these. Fold them into the beats rather than appending them.

- **Who this is for / who it is not for.** The "not for" list is counterintuitively persuasive: it proves you are not desperate for everyone, and it lets the right reader self-select hard. Alma de Tüz pages already do this ("esto no le sirve a todo el mundo").
- **Practical facts, unmissable.** Date, place, duration, price, group size, what to bring. A reader who wants to come and cannot find the price leaves.
- **Objections, answered as questions.** Collect the real ones from the user's brief. Typical: "no sé cantar / coser", "es caro", "no tengo tiempo", "voy sola y me da vergüenza", "¿y si no me sale nada?". Answer in the reader's own framing, briefly, without defensiveness.
- **Risk reversal.** Guarantee, refund policy, or - when neither exists - a plain description of what happens if they change their mind. Say what is true.
- **What happens after I click.** One line. Removes the fear of an irreversible commitment, which is the single biggest silent objection on email-capture pages.
